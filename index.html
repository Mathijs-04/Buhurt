I The Sport fol. 1r

Buhurt is medieval combat, reborn as a modern full-contact sport. Teams of knights in full harness walk into a wooden arena, the list, and try to put each other on the ground with blunted steel, shields and bare strength. Nothing is scored and nothing is staged. The side with fighters still standing wins.

¶ The word

From the Old French béhourd: a rough, informal kind of medieval tournament in which groups of knights clashed with blunted weapons, instead of riding at each other one pair at a time.

¶ The revival

Steel combat came back in Russia, Ukraine and Belarus around the turn of the millennium. In 2010 four national teams fought the first Battle of the Nations at Khotyn Fortress in Ukraine.

¶ The sport today

National teams from around thirty countries now meet at world championships. In July 2026 the IMCF world championship filled Spøttrup Castle in Denmark with close to six hundred fighters.

✠ Chapter II

Rules

Six laws of the list.
Break one and the marshals step in.

Scroll
I

Stay standing

Two feet on the ground is fighting. A third point of contact (a knee, a hand, a shoulder) means you are down and out for the round.

Rule of three points
II

Never thrust

Thrusts are banned. Swords, axes and poleaxes are swung, hooked and bashed, never driven point-first like a spear.

Percussion only
III

Mind the forbidden

The neck and throat, the groin, the back of the knees, the feet and ankles are off limits, and so is anyone already down. Some leagues also protect the back of the head and the spine.

Prohibited targets
IV

Wrestle freely

Grabs, trips, throws and shield-bashes are all legal. Plenty of knights hit the sand because of a shove, not a sword.

Grappling permitted
V

No points, only survivors

In a melee nobody counts hits. The round ends when one side has no fighters left standing.

Last team standing
VI

Be true to the age

Armour has to be based on historical sources, matched in region and period. Kits are inspected for safety and authenticity before anyone fights.

Authenticity check

IV The Armour fol. 4v

Anatomy
of a knight

A buhurt harness is dozens of hardened steel plates, riveted and strapped over a padded gambeson. It has to take a full-power poleaxe swing and still let you wrestle.

Weight carried 30 kg
  • BascinetClosed helmet of hardened steel, heavily padded inside
  • AventailRiveted mail hung from the helmet over the neck
  • SpaulderPlates over the shoulder
  • RerebraceUpper-arm plate
  • CouterThe elbow cop
  • BrigandineSteel plates riveted inside a fabric coat
  • VambraceForearm tube
  • GambesonQuilted padding worn under everything

  • CuisseThigh defence
