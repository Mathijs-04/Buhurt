# Buhurt site: content brief

You are gathering images and a few facts for a private, non-public fan website about **buhurt** (modern full-contact armoured combat with blunted steel weapons). The site is already designed and built. Your job is **content only**: find the images, save them in the right folders, and record where each one came from. **Do not edit `index.html`, `styles.css` or `main.js`.** The images are integrated afterwards.

Copyright is not a blocker here: this is a private prototype and nothing will be published. Still record the source and owner of every file in the manifest, because the footer credits "their respective owners".

---

## How to deliver

1. Save files in `assets/drop/<folder>/` (folders already exist): `weapons/`, `armour/`, `helmets/`, `gallery/`, `details/`.
2. Name files in lowercase kebab-case after what they show, e.g. `weapons/poleaxe.jpg`, `helmets/klappvisor-bascinet.png`, `gallery/shield-wall-01.jpg`.
3. Use JPG, PNG or WebP. Always download the **largest version available**: open the full-size image or the product zoom, never a thumbnail or screenshot.
4. Fill in `assets/drop/MANIFEST.md` (create it) with one row per file:

   | File | What it shows | Source page URL | Direct image URL | Owner / photographer | Pixel size |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|

5. Finish with a short list of anything you could not find, and why.

---

## Image quality rules (apply to everything)

- **Modern buhurt gear only.** Show equipment made for and used in today's sport, not museum pieces, film props, LARP foam, or fantasy or AI-generated art.
- **Real photographs only.** No illustrations, renders or AI images.
- **No heavy watermarks** across the subject. A small photographer logo in a corner is fine.
- **Sharp and well lit.** Skip blurry, very dark or heavily filtered shots.
- **Minimum sizes:** product shots ≥ 1500 px on the long edge; the full-armour shot ≥ 2000 px tall; gallery shots ≥ 2000 px long edge where possible. 1080 px is acceptable only when it's an exceptional shot and no bigger version exists.

## To-do 3: Full armour kit → `assets/drop/armour/`

**What the site does with it:** an "Anatomy of a knight" section shows one fighter head to toe, with labelled call-out lines pointing at each piece of armour. The background is removed automatically.

**Needed:**
- **One main photo:** a complete modern buhurt harness, **standing, full body, head to toe**, front or three-quarter view. A plain background works best: a maker's full-kit product photo on a mannequin or model is ideal. ≥ 2000 px tall.
- **The parts list for that exact kit**, in the manifest. Use the maker's names where given, e.g. helmet + aventail, gorget, brigandine or cuirass, spaulders, rerebraces, couters, vambraces, gauntlets, tassets, cuisses, poleyns, greaves, sabatons, and the gambeson underneath.
- **The kit's total weight**, if the maker states it.
- Optional: 2–3 alternative full-body kits (different periods or styles), in case the first one doesn't cut out cleanly.

## To-do 4: Gallery → `assets/drop/gallery/`

**What the site does with them:** a three-column photo gallery, "From the List", with a full-screen view on click. This is the showcase, so pick the **most dramatic shots** you can find.

**Needed:** 15–25 action photos in a mix of portrait and landscape:
- big mass battles (5v5, 12v12, larger), with lots of fighters in frame
- impact moments: strikes landing, sparks, shields colliding, polearm sweeps
- takedowns and throws: a fighter going down, dust flying
- duels with weapons crossed
- the crowd and arena atmosphere around the list
- a few intimate shots: a fighter between rounds, a dented helmet, a team lining up

**Where to look:**
- **Instagram @buhurtinraw** has excellent action shots and is the first place to look. Instagram usually needs a logged-in account and serves 1080 px at most. Grab the largest version, and note the post URL so the photographer can be asked for the original later.
- Other buhurt photographers on Instagram (search hashtags like #buhurt, #buhurtphotography, #medievalcombat, #armoredcombat).
- Websites and galleries of federations and events: IMCF world championships, Buhurt Prime, Buhurt International, and national leagues.
- News photo galleries covering IMCF world championships and big buhurt events (agency photos are very high quality).
- Flickr and photographer portfolio sites, which often have full-resolution originals.
